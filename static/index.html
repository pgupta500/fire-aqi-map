Fires and bad air often sit in the same places, but that alone doesn't show who is responsible. SmokeTrail follows the wind.

1 · Trace each monitor's air backwards

Starting at every PM2.5 monitor at the time of its reading, we step back hour by hour through the wind field (a back-trajectory, the same idea as NOAA's HYSPLIT model) to find where that air was over the previous 24–48 hours.

2 · Credit the fires along the path

A fire is credited when the air passed close to it at about the time it was burning. Bigger fires (higher radiative power), closer passes and fresher smoke count more:

smoke(fire → monitor) = FRP × exp(−d² / 2σ²) σ = (25 km + 1 km/h × hours back) / 2 × exp(−Δt² / 2·6h²) fire burning when air passed × exp(−hours back / 36h) dilution

3 · Roll up by region

Each fire is placed in its Indian state, Pakistani province, Bangladeshi division or country. Summing the smoke that reached monitors in other regions gives each region's exported smoke and who received it.

Limits

  • One transport layer (average of 10 m and ~750 m winds on a 2° grid); no plume rise, chemistry or rain-out.
  • Satellites miss small, cloud-covered or short-lived fires.
  • PM2.5 also comes from traffic, industry, dust and household burning, which this map doesn't model.
  • Treat it as a strong pointer to where smoke came from, not a court-grade source apportionment.
